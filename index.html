How I work

Simulate → Build → Validate

Every project on this page runs the same loop. The interesting engineering is in the last step — the gap between what the model promised and what the hardware actually did.

  1. 01 — Simulate

    Model it first

    Work out the aerodynamic, structural or electrical behaviour before anything is cut, printed or wired. Cheap iterations happen here.

    Where I've done it

    • VAWT rotor analysis & optimisation
    • Motor & battery-pack sizing at Avironix
    • MEng thesis — aerofoil under varying wind loads
    • E-Kart powertrain calculations

  2. 02 — Build

    Put it in your hands

    CAD to physical part: additive manufacturing, fabrication, wiring, assembly. A design that can't be built isn't a design yet.

    Where I've done it

    • Propulsion test rig — designed & fabricated
    • Falcon 1–3 autonomous multirotors
    • Functional parts at XTRUDE3D
    • Baja ATV frame fabrication & wiring

  3. 03 — Validate

    Find out where it lied

    Test rigs, wind tunnels, instrumented flights. Measure the real thing and compare it honestly with the prediction.

    Where I've done it

    • Wind-tunnel testing against prediction
    • Bench-testing propulsion on the rig
    • Calibration, PID tuning & test flights
Iterate — feed the test back into the model

Wind energyJan – Apr 2026

H-type Darrieus vertical-axis wind turbine

A straight-bladed Darrieus rotor, taken from design and analysis through optimisation to wind-tunnel testing. The brief set two objectives — and the second is what this whole portfolio is about.

Scope
Design, analysis, optimisation, experimental validation
Period
Jan – Apr 2026
Test
Wind tunnel, varying loads
Loop
  • Sim
  • Build
  • Test

The brief

  1. Maximise power output under varying loads.
  2. Minimise the deviation between predicted and experimental performance.

The first objective is a design problem. The second is a modelling problem: it asks how good the prediction was, not just how good the turbine was.

Why it's hard

An H-rotor blade never sits at one angle of attack. As it travels round, the flow it sees swings through a large range every revolution — and at low tip-speed ratios that swing goes well past the angle where a static aerofoil stalls. Move the slider.

Method

  1. 01 Simulate

    Analysed the rotor and optimised the design for power output across the load range.

  2. 02 Build

    Produced the rotor as a physical test article for the tunnel.

  3. 03 Validate

    Tested under varying loads and compared measured performance against the prediction.

Rotor

Straight blades on struts, turning about a vertical shaft: the rotor accepts wind from any direction, at the cost of the cyclic loading shown above. Schematic only.

Connects to

This sits right next to my MEng thesis at DCU — simulating the aerodynamic interaction of an aerofoil under different wind loads, for a novel wind energy system.

Case study 02 / 04

UAV propulsionJul 2024 – Apr 2025

Hybrid UAV propulsion test stand

Engineering intern, Avironix Drones · BE thesis, LICET

Sizing a UAV's propulsion and power system is a prediction built from datasheets and calculations. At Avironix I did that sizing for different aircraft — then designed, analysed, fabricated and tested a propulsion test rig so the predictions could be checked on the bench before anything flew.

Role
Engineering intern
Period
Jul 2024 – Apr 2025
Output
Test rig + BE thesis
Loop
  • Sim
  • Build
  • Test

The problem

Motor, propeller and battery are chosen together, and every choice trades against the others — endurance against payload against mass against thermal headroom. Datasheet figures are measured under someone else's conditions. A test stand is how you find out what your combination actually delivers.

What I did

  • Sized and selected motors and battery packs for different UAVs.
  • Designed and analysed a propulsion-system test rig.
  • Fabricated the rig and tested propulsion configurations on it.
  • Wrote it up as my undergraduate thesis: Design and Development of Hybrid UAV Propulsion System Test Stand.

Method

  1. 01 Simulate

    Size motor, propeller and battery pack from the requirement and datasheets.

  2. 02 Build

    Design, analyse and fabricate the test rig.

  3. 03 Validate

    Bench-test the combinations and compare against the sizing.
